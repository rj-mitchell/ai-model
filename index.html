AI Transformation Model

Each year the static equation gives a snapshot of every sector and the whole economy. Two update rules then feed that snapshot back into next year's useful AI work T and each sector's workflow redesign gi. Move the dials to see whether the economy compounds, stalls, or drowns in slop.

The equations

The note under each equation gives the value that switches each optional mechanism off.

1 · Sector level: net real economic value

\[f_i^*(T)=f_{i,\max}-(f_{i,\max}-f_{i,0})\,T^{-\beta} \qquad \epsilon_i=(1-a_i)(1-g_i)\]
\[\tau_i=\epsilon_i+\frac{1-\epsilon_i}{A\,T_{\text{eff}}} \qquad \tau_{i,0}=\epsilon_{i,0}+\frac{1-\epsilon_{i,0}}{A},\;\; \epsilon_{i,0}=1-a_0 f_{i,0} \qquad S_i=\frac{\tau_{i,0}}{\tau_i}\]
\[V_i=\frac{1+p_i\,(S_i-1)}{1+\big(\eta_iT^{-\delta_\eta}\ln T_{\text{eff}}+\gamma_iT^{-\delta_\gamma}\ln T\big)\,S_i}\]

Available native fit \(f_i^*\) rises with capability toward a fixed ceiling (\(\beta=0\) keeps it at \(f_{i,0}\)). \(a_i\) is the native fit actually in use; the snapshot mode assumes full adoption (\(a_i=f_i^*\)). \(\tau\) is time per unit of work relative to doing it without AI, and \(A\) is how much faster today's AI already is on the work it is used for. Speedup \(S_i\) is measured against how work was really done at end-2026, so adopting or redesigning around today's AI pays off even if AI stops improving (\(A=1\) switches this off). Review burden falls as AI gets more reliable (\(\delta_\eta=0\) off), and defensive AI offsets external attack (\(\delta_\gamma=0\) off). Deployment rule: Private sectors use \(T_{\text{eff}}=\min(T,T^*)\), where the Slop Peak \(T^*\) is the usage level that maximises \(V_i\); commons use \(T_{\text{eff}}=T\). Native fit is capped at 95% and redesign at 90%.

2 · Macro level: Core, Flex and new products

\[V_{\text{core}}=\Big(\sum_{i\in\text{Core}}\tilde w_i V_i^{\rho}\Big)^{1/\rho} \qquad V_{\text{flex}}=\sum_{j\in\text{Flex}}\tilde w_j V_j\]
\[D=\min(1,V_{\text{core}})^{\kappa} \qquad V_{\text{macro}}=\Big(W_{\text{core}}V_{\text{core}}^{\rho_m}+W_{\text{flex}}\big(D\,V_{\text{flex}}\big)^{\rho_m}\Big)^{1/\rho_m}+D\,n_t\]

Core carries exactly \(W_{\text{core}}\) of any change in the total. \(\rho_m<0\) makes Core and Flex complements, so spending shifts toward the slower tier (the Baumol effect). \(D\) scales Flex down only when Core is below baseline. \(n_t\) is AI-native products. A tier with no sectors is left out.

3 · Dynamic loop: tech power

\[x_t=r_0\,\big(V_{\text{R\&D},t}\big)^{\phi}\,\min(1,V_{\text{core},t})\,\frac{1+1/T_{\text{half}}}{1+T_t/T_{\text{half}}}\]
\[\text{growth}_t=\frac{x_t}{\big(1+(x_t/\text{cap})^4\big)^{1/4}} \qquad T_{t+\Delta t}=T_t\,(1+\text{growth}_t)^{\Delta t}\]

The \(1+1/T_{\text{half}}\) factor makes year-one growth equal \(r_0\). The speed limit leaves ordinary growth untouched and bends very fast growth toward the cap, standing in for how fast chips, data centres and power can be built. The model steps quarterly (\(\Delta t=\tfrac14\)) and reports yearly. Setback: in the chosen year, \(T\) is multiplied by \((1-\text{size})\), with a floor of 1.

4 · Dynamic loop: adoption, redesign, new products, risk

\[a_{i,t+1}=a_{i,t}+\lambda\big(f_i^*(T_t)-a_{i,t}\big),\quad a_{i,0}=a_0\,f_{i,0}\]
\[g_{i,t+1}=g_{i,t}+\alpha_i\Big(\frac{g_{i,\max}}{1+(T_{\text{crit},i}/T_t)^{k}}-g_{i,t}\Big)\]
\[n_{t+1}=n_t+s\big(N_{\max}H(T_t,T_{\text{new}})-n_t\big)\]
\[\text{survival}_{t+1}=\text{survival}_t\big(1-h_{\max}H(T_t,T_{\text{risk}})\big)\]

Rates \(\lambda,\alpha_i,s\) are shares of the gap closed per year, applied per quarter as \(1-(1-\text{rate})^{1/4}\). \(k=2.5\). \(H(T,m)=\frac{T^k-1}{T^k-1+m^k}\) is an S-curve that is 0 at \(T=1\) and reaches ½ near \(m\). The risk-adjusted economy is \(V_{\text{macro}}\,\big(1-(1-\text{survival})\times\text{loss}\big)\).

T
Useful AI work: verified work per £ on the compressible slice of a job, as a multiple of end-2026 (1×). Not raw tokens.
A
Today's AI speedup: how much faster end-2026 AI does the tasks it is used for than people without it.
fi*, ai
Native fit: the share of the workflow AI can compress (f*, rising from f₀ toward the fixed ceiling f_max), and the share actually being done with AI (a, which lags behind).
gi
Gustafson redesign: share of the remaining bottleneck removed by re-architecting the workflow (capped at 90%).
pi
Real wealth share: 1 means the speedup creates wealth, 0 means a zero-sum Red Queen arms race.
ηi, γi
Internal slop and ambient exposure (slider 0–10 maps to 0–0.5).
ρ, ρm
Complementarity inside Core (ρ = 1 − fragility), and between Core and Flex (ρm = 1 − complementarity).
w̃, W
Sector weights within each tier (normalised), and the Core / Flex split of the 2026 economy.

What this model still can't tell you

  • It is not calibrated. About 80 inputs are judgement calls, and the results depend mostly on native fit, the wealth share and the slop settings. Use the uncertainty band, and treat results as scenarios, not forecasts.
  • Jobs and wages are not modelled. "Tasks automated" measures how much work AI handles. Whether that means lost jobs, higher wages or shorter hours depends on a labour market this model doesn't contain. Adding one would double the number of unanchored inputs.
  • Catastrophic risk is an overlay you set. It discounts the economy but doesn't feed back into AI progress, and the yearly hazard rising with T is an assumption, not a finding.
  • Red Queen sectors lose from every speedup. When a sector's real-wealth share p is below a/(1 + a), where a is its slop term (η ln T_eff + γ ln T), anything that makes it faster lowers its value. That is the intended arms-race logic, but it is a strong claim. The sector status flags when a sector is in this regime.
  • New products, capability breadth and defensive AI are the most speculative mechanisms. They have the least evidence behind them and push results up. Setting them to zero gives a deliberately conservative model.
  • Strong Core–Flex complementarity makes a whole-tier collapse costly. With high complementarity, a collapse of the entire Flex tier drags the economy down. A single Flex sector collapsing still doesn't, because sectors inside Flex are averaged normally.
  • Some limits are irreducible by design. Native fit, redesign and new products all have fixed ceilings, so the economy levels off however capable AI becomes. That is deliberate: the model shows which bottlenecks capability alone does not remove. Raising the ceilings is a different claim, not a correction.
  • The uncertainty band covers inputs, not structure. It varies every setting, including the structural dials, with errors partly shared across sectors. It cannot cover equations that are wrong in form. The headline shows the median of the runs, which sits below the central run because outcomes are curved and several inputs start at their upper limits.
  • Each run is deterministic apart from the uncertainty runs. Shocks other than the single setback, business cycles and policy responses are not represented.
