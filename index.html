AI Transformation Model

How much richer could AI make an advanced economy, and why doesn't it explode? Pick a scenario or move the dials to see whether output compounds, stalls, or is eaten up by errors and arms races.

A simplified model. Six broad sectors, each 13–20% of GDP, sized from US data as a template for advanced economies. Settings come from published evidence where it exists, with judgement filling the gaps, so read the results as scenarios, not forecasts.

    How the model works

    The equations, for anyone who wants to check them

    What the numbers measure: every figure is a multiple of a world where AI stays at its end-2026 level and use. GDP counts all output, including activity that is only an arms race and spending on defence; useful output leaves those out, along with the value people lose when human service they preferred is replaced. Both include the extra capital that higher output pays for. The GDP chart adds 2.0% a year of growth from everything except AI to both worlds, with 2027 = 1. Settings without a dial are listed under "Fixed assumptions", with their evidence.

    1 · Each sector

    \[f_i^*=f_{i,0}+(f_{i,\max}+e_{i,t}-f_{i,0})\,c_t,\;\; e_{i,t}=0.15\,(1-f_{i,\max})\,H^\circ(E_t,20)\,(1-C) \qquad a_i\le f_i^* \qquad g_i\le g_{i,\max}\]
    \[\tau_i=(1-a_i)+a_i(1-g_i)\Big(\hat h_t+\frac{1-\hat h_t}{\hat A E_t}\Big)+\frac{a_i g_i}{\hat A E_t}+\hat\kappa_i a_i \qquad S_i=\frac{\tau_{i,0}}{\tau_i}\]
    \[G_i=\ell_i\big(1-E_t^{-\nu}\big),\;\; L_i=G_i(1-D\,c_t),\;\; d_i=0.5\,G_i D\,c_t \qquad \hat p_i=p_i(1-r\,c_t),\;\; \tilde p_i=\hat p_i+(1-\hat p_i)\,C\]
    \[V^{\text{GDP}}_i=S_i(1-L_i) \qquad V_i=\big(1+\tilde p_i(S_i-1)\big)\,(1-L_i-d_i)\,\Big(1-0.5\,e_{i,t}\tfrac{a_i}{f_i^*}\Big)\]

    \(c_t\in[0,1)\) is AI's capability: the share of the gap to each sector's ceiling it has closed, reaching 0.9 in the AGI year. \(E_t\) is useful AI work per dollar (end-2026 = 1). \(f_i^*\) is the work AI can do, \(a_i\) the work done with AI, and \(g_i\) the share of that rebuilt to run without people directing it. \(e_{i,t}\) is work that was human by choice and has been competed away: 15% of the work out of AI's reach can go, half of it once AI is about 20 times cheaper (\(H^\circ\), an S-curve), less with coordination \(C\). In \(\tau_i\), time per unit of work: untouched work, AI-assisted work where a share \(\hat h_t=h(1-0.75\,c_t)\) stays human, rebuilt work, and checking, \(\hat\kappa_i=\kappa_i(1-0.5\,c_t)\), people's time verifying AI's output, which doesn't shrink as AI gets cheaper. \(\hat A\) is AI's own speed implied by today's 1.4× speedup, and \(\tau_{i,0}\) is how work was done at end-2026. Fraud and attack pressure \(G_i\) rises as cheaper AI makes attacks cheaper (\(\nu=0.5\)); defensive AI removes up to half of it (\(D=0.5\)), leaving loss \(L_i\) and costing \(d_i\) in defence spending, which GDP counts but useful output doesn't. \(p_i\) is the share of the speedup that creates real wealth rather than feeding a race; \(r=0\) by default (races hold their share) and 0.5 in the "Races deepen" scenario. Coordination \(C\) is the coordination dial times 0.5, because half of it leaks into other channels. Replacing human-by-choice work loses half its value to people, which only useful output records.

    2 · The whole economy

    \[V_{\text{ess}}=\Big(\sum_{\text{ess}}\tilde w_i V_i^{\rho}\Big)^{\frac1\rho} \qquad V_{\text{flex}}=\Big(\sum_{\text{flex}}\tilde w_j V_j^{\rho_f}\Big)^{\frac1{\rho_f}}\]
    \[Y_t=\Big[\Big(W_{\text{ess}}V_{\text{ess}}^{\rho_m}+W_{\text{flex}}V_{\text{flex}}^{\rho_m}\Big)^{\frac1{\rho_m}}+n_t\Big]\,k_t^{\alpha}\,(1-\varrho_t),\;\; \varrho_t=0.02\,\text{coord}\,\big(1-e^{-t/30}\big)\]

    \(\tilde w\) are sector shares of GDP within each group and \(W_{\text{ess}}\) the essential sectors' share (about two-thirds). \(\rho=-4\) (\(\sigma\approx0.2\)) makes the weakest essential sector hold the rest back; \(\rho_f=1/3\) (\(\sigma=1.5\)) lets flexible sectors partly stand in for each other; \(\rho_m=-0.5\) means people need both groups, so spending shifts toward whichever AI speeds up least. \(n_t\) is AI-native products and \(k_t\) the capital stock relative to no further AI, with capital's share \(\alpha=0.35\). \(\varrho_t\) is the cost of the rules that hold races back, which builds up over decades, reaching 2% of output at full coordination. GDP uses \(V^{\text{GDP}}_i\), useful output \(V_i\).

    3 · How fast AI improves

    \[F_t=V_{\text{R\&D},t}^{\,\phi}\;Y_t^{\,\lambda} \qquad E_{t+\Delta t}=E_t\big(1+r_0\,F_t\,E_t^{-\theta}\big)^{\Delta t} \qquad \ell_{t+\Delta t}=\ell_t+F_t\Delta t,\;\; c_t=1-e^{-k_c\ell_t}\]

    The economy feeds back into AI progress two ways: a more productive AI research sector (knowledge and professional services, \(\phi=0.4\)), and higher output, including from the essential sectors that build chips, power and data centres, which funds more compute and research (\(\lambda=0.5\)). Progress gets harder as AI improves (\(\theta=0.22\), from evidence that research productivity falls about 5% a year). Capability \(c_t\) advances with the same feedback; \(k_c\) is set so it reaches 0.9 in the AGI year. The model steps quarterly and reports yearly.

    4 · Adoption, rebuilding and capital

    \[\dot a_i=\lambda_a m_t\,a_i\Big(1-\frac{a_i}{f_i^*}\Big),\;\; a_{i,0}=0.2\,f_{i,0} \qquad \dot g_i=\alpha_i m_t\Big(g_{i,\max}\big[1-(1-H^*_i)(1-c_t)\big]-g_i\Big),\; H^*_i=\frac{1}{1+(T_{\text{crit},i}/E_t)^{2.5}}\]
    \[\dot n=0.15\,m_t\big(N_{\max}H^\circ(E_t,10)-n\big) \qquad \dot k=\delta\,(Y_t-k)\]
    \[m_t=\Big(\frac{\ln R_t}{\ln 1.4}\Big)^{0.25},\; R_t=\Big(\hat h_t+\frac{1-\hat h_t}{\hat A E_t}\Big)^{-1} \qquad V_i\leftarrow V_i\big(1-1.5\max(0,\dot u_i-\dot u_{i,0})\big)\]

    Use of AI spreads along an S-curve, and adoption, rebuilding and new products all move faster as AI's advantage \(R_t\) grows. Rebuilding becomes worth doing once AI is either cheap enough (\(H^*\)) or capable enough (\(c_t\)). Capital catches up with output at the depreciation rate \(\delta=5\%\), so the boost from capital builds over decades. The last term is the up-front cost of change: while AI's share of work, \(u_i=a_i(1+g_i)\), rises faster than today's pace, some output goes into training and reorganising. Yearly rates are applied per quarter. \(H^\circ(E,m)=\frac{E^{2.5}-1}{E^{2.5}-1+m^{2.5}}\).

    What this model can't tell you

    • It is a sketch, not a forecast. Six broad sectors stand in for a whole economy, each blending industries with very different settings. Sizes come from data; how much is an arms race, checking time and rebuilding speed are judgement anchored on evidence, with wide ranges. The shaded band shows how much the results move when every setting is varied by about ±25%; it can't catch equations that are wrong in form.
    • It is sized like the US. Manufacturing-heavy economies such as Germany and Japan have a larger physical sector, which lowers their gains only slightly here. It is not a model of the world economy, where farming, informal work and slower adoption would change the picture.
    • Robotics is only partly included. Ceilings on hands-on work allow for robots that use demonstrated technology (about 13% of US work hours, McKinsey 2025), but not for a robotics breakthrough. The "Robotics breakthrough" scenario shows what changes if one comes.
    • Some limits are fixed on purpose. The ceilings on what AI can do, on rebuilding and on new products don't rise however capable AI becomes, so the economy levels off. That is the point: it shows which bottlenecks capability alone doesn't remove. The explainer shows what each limit costs, and what happens if they are all removed.
    • Capital and workers are treated simply. Capital is one economy-wide stock that catches up with output, and each sector keeps its starting share of workers. Who earns the gains is not modelled, though it may matter most: if AI replaces human work, people's preferences may carry less weight in what the economy produces.
    • Races, human-by-choice work and coordination rest on thin evidence. Race spending has held a steady share of GDP for decades, so that is the default; whether AI changes that is a scenario. How much human service competition can take, what that loss is worth and what coordination costs are judgement anchored on a handful of cases.
    • Jobs, wages and catastrophic risk are not modelled. "Tasks done with AI" measures work, not jobs. The model asks what AI does to output if things broadly go right; it has no shocks, business cycles or policy responses.
