    How the model works

    The equations, for anyone who wants to check them

    What the numbers measure: every output figure is a multiple of output without further AI, meaning a world where AI stays at its end-2026 level and use. 1.6× means 60% more output than that world. The GDP chart adds 2.0% a year of growth from everything except AI to both worlds: \(\text{GDP}_t=V_{t}\,(1.02)^t\) with AI and \((1.02)^t\) without, with 2027 = 1. Settings without a dial are listed under "Fixed assumptions".

    1 · Each sector's output

    \[f_i^*(T)=f_{i,\max}-(f_{i,\max}-f_{i,0})\,T^{-\beta} \qquad \epsilon_i=(1-a_i)(1-g_i)\]
    \[\tau_i=\epsilon_i+\underbrace{a_i(1-g_i)}_{\text{assisted}}\Big(h+\frac{1-h}{\hat A\,T_{\text{eff}}}\Big)+\underbrace{g_i}_{\text{rebuilt}}\frac{1}{\hat A\,T_{\text{eff}}} \qquad \frac{1}{A}=h+\frac{1-h}{\hat A}\]
    \[\tau_{i,0}=\epsilon_{i,0}+\frac{1-\epsilon_{i,0}}{A},\;\; \epsilon_{i,0}=1-a_0 f_{i,0} \qquad S_i=\frac{\tau_{i,0}}{\tau_i}\]
    \[V_i=\frac{1+p_i\,(S_i-1)}{1+\big(\hat\eta_i\ln T_{\text{eff}}+\gamma_iT^{-\delta_\gamma}\ln T\big)\,S_i}\]

    \(f_i^*\) is the share of a sector's work AI can do, rising with capability \(T\) toward a fixed ceiling; \(\beta\) is set so it has closed 90% of the gap by the AGI year. \(a_i\) is the share actually done with AI. \(\epsilon_i\) is the work AI doesn't touch. AI-assisted work in old setups keeps a share \(h\) human, so it gets at most \(1/h\) faster; work rebuilt around AI (\(g_i\)) speeds up fully. \(\tau\) is time per unit of work, \(A\) today's speedup on assisted work, and \(S_i\) the speedup against how work was done at end-2026. \(p_i\) is the share of the speedup that creates real wealth rather than feeding an arms race. \(\eta_i\) (checking costs inside the sector, falling as AI broadens: \(\hat\eta_i=\eta_i(1-0.5\,c_t)\), \(c_t=1-T^{-\beta}\)) and \(\gamma_i\) (spam, fraud and attack from outside, partly offset by defensive AI, \(\delta_\gamma=0.1\)) are scored 0–10 and divided by 200. Each sector uses AI only up to the level that maximises its output, \(T_{\text{eff}}=\min(T,T^*)\); outside attacks scale with the full \(T\). AI-ready work is capped at 95% and rebuilding at 90%.

    2 · The whole economy

    \[V_{\text{ess}}=\Big(\sum_{i\in\text{ess}}\tilde w_i V_i^{\rho}\Big)^{1/\rho} \qquad V_{\text{flex}}=\sum_{j\in\text{flex}}\tilde w_j V_j\]
    \[V=\Big(W_{\text{ess}}V_{\text{ess}}^{\rho_m}+W_{\text{flex}}V_{\text{flex}}^{\rho_m}\Big)^{1/\rho_m}+n_t\]

    \(\tilde w\) are sector shares within each group and \(W_{\text{ess}}\) the essential sectors' share of GDP (about two-thirds). \(\rho=-4\) makes the weakest essential sector dominate. \(\rho_m=-0.5\) means people need both groups, so spending shifts toward whichever AI speeds up least. \(n_t\) is AI-native products.

    3 · How fast AI improves

    \[x_t=r_0\,\big(V_{\text{R\&D},t}\big)^{\phi}\,\min(1,V_{\text{ess},t})\,\frac{1+1/T_{\text{half}}}{1+T_t/T_{\text{half}}}\]
    \[\text{growth}_t=\frac{x_t}{\big(1+(x_t/\text{cap})^4\big)^{1/4}} \qquad T_{t+\Delta t}=T_t\,(1+\text{growth}_t)^{\Delta t}\]

    \(T\) is useful AI work: verified work per dollar, end-2026 = 1. It grows at \(r_0\) in year one, faster when the AI research sector (knowledge and professional services) is more productive (\(\phi=0.4\)), slower when essential sectors fall below baseline, and with diminishing returns (\(T_{\text{half}}=15\)). A speed limit of 200% a year stands in for how fast chips, data centres and power can be built. The model steps quarterly and reports yearly.

    4 · Adoption, rebuilding and new products

    \[\dot a_i=\lambda\,m_t\,a_i\Big(1-\frac{a_i}{f_i^*(T)}\Big),\quad a_{i,0}=0.2\,f_{i,0}\]
    \[g_{i,t+1}=g_{i,t}+\alpha_i m_t\Big(\frac{g_{i,\max}}{1+(T_{\text{crit},i}/T_t)^{2.5}}-g_{i,t}\Big) \qquad n_{t+1}=n_t+0.15\,m_t\big(N_{\max}H(T_t,10)-n_t\big)\]
    \[m_t=\Big(\frac{\ln R_t}{\ln 1.4}\Big)^{0.25},\quad R_t=\Big(h+\frac{1-h}{\hat A\,T_t}\Big)^{-1} \qquad V_i\leftarrow V_i\big(1-0.25\max(0,\dot u_i-\dot u_{i,0})\big)\]

    Use of AI spreads along an S-curve; adoption, rebuilding and new products all move faster as AI's advantage \(R_t\) grows. The last term is the up-front cost of change: while the share of work done with AI, \(u_i=1-\epsilon_i\), rises faster than today's pace, some output goes into training and reorganising, so gains arrive later. Yearly rates are applied per quarter. \(H(T,m)=\frac{T^{2.5}-1}{T^{2.5}-1+m^{2.5}}\) is an S-curve that is 0 at \(T=1\) and ½ near \(m\).

    What this model can't tell you

    • It is a sketch, not a forecast. Six broad sectors stand in for a whole economy, each blending industries with very different settings. Sizes come from data; how much is an arms race, checking costs and rebuilding speed are judgement anchored on evidence, with wide ranges. The shaded band shows how much the results move when every setting is varied by about ±25%; it can't catch equations that are wrong in form.
    • It is sized like the US. Manufacturing-heavy economies such as Germany and Japan have a larger physical sector, which lowers their gains only slightly here. It is not a model of the world economy, where farming, informal work and slower adoption would change the picture.
    • Robotics is only partly included. Ceilings on hands-on work allow for robots that use demonstrated technology (about 13% of US work hours, McKinsey 2025), but not for a robotics breakthrough. The "Robotics breakthrough" scenario shows what changes if one comes.
    • Some limits are fixed on purpose. The ceilings on what AI can do, on rebuilding and on new products don't rise however capable AI becomes, so the economy levels off. That is the point: it shows which bottlenecks capability alone doesn't remove. Raising the ceilings is a different claim, not a correction.
    • Arms-race sectors lose from every speedup. Where most of a sector's activity is competition rather than new wealth, making it faster lowers its value. The sector status flags when that happens.
    • Jobs, wages and catastrophic risk are not modelled. "Tasks done with AI" measures work, not jobs. The model asks what AI does to output if things broadly go right; it has no shocks, business cycles or policy responses.
